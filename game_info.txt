Mechanics:
Planting Pearl Dummies: player clicks an empty clam slot to plant a nucleus/dummy bead, initiating the biological growing process.
Cleaning Build-up (Scrub Brush): over time, algae and sediment accumulate on active clams, slowing down growth. The player must manually scrub clams using a free brush tool to maintain optimal production speeds.
Nutrient Chum (Speed Boost): unlocked at Level 2. The player throws chum into a clam bed to temporarily accelerate pearl growth. 
Crabs (The Threat): unlocked at Level 3. Drawn by the scent/activity of high-speed growth, crabs crawl toward active clams. If a crab reaches an open clam, it pries it open and damages the growing pearl, destroying all current progress and forcing the player to clean the clam and re-grow the pearl from scratch.
Sonic flares (The Defense): unlocked at Level 3. A limited-use active tool that emits underwater acoustic shockwaves to scare off approaching crabs before they can reach a clam.
Harvesting & Selling: once a pearl fully matures, the player gently harvests it from the clam and takes it to surface buyers for cash and large XP gains.
Core gameplay loop
[Plant Pearl Dummy] →[Clean Build-Up / Apply Chum] → [Scent Spawns Crab] 
                    |                                                                                                           |
[Harvest & Sell at Surface] ←  [Matures] ← [Scare Crab with Sonic Flare] 
Initiate Growth: Plant a dummy bead in an open clam shell.
Accelerate & Maintain: Scrub off sediment build-up to keep growth running smoothly, or apply Chum to speed up growth.
Defend: If Chum draws a Crab, deploy a Sonic Flare before it pries open the clam and ruins the pearl.
Harvest & Cash In: Collect the completed pearl and bring it to the surface to sell, earning cash and XP to level up.


Stage 1: Teach the player the mechanics
Goal: Learn every core mechanic slowly through milestone-based XP leveling, while fulfilling the first order. 
Level 1: The player starts with 1 clam plot. They plant a pearl dummy, keep it clean from build-up using the Scrub Brush, and harvest their very first pearl.
Milestone: Harvesting Pearl #1 grants enough XP to reach Level 2.
Level 2: Reaching Level 2 unlocks Nutrient Chum. The player is prompted to plant a second pearl dummy and apply Chum to see accelerated growth in action.
Milestone: Through micro-actions (applying chum, scrubbing) and harvesting Pearl #2, the player earns enough XP to reach Level 3.
Level 3: Reaching Level 3 unlocks Crabs and Sonic Flares. A crab spawns and targets the third growing clam. The player must deploy a Sonic Flare to scare the crab away, protect the pearl, and harvest it.
Stage 1 Completion: The player brings all 3 harvested pearls to the surface dock and sells them to the untimed first buyer. Selling the batch awards a massive XP boost that pushes the player to Level 4 and unlocks Stage 2.
Stage 2: Freeplay
Freeplay begins the instant the player reaches Level 5, and leveling never really stops after that -- it just changes shape. From Level 5 onward, XP requirements grow level over level rather than sitting at the fixed pearl-count thresholds Stage 1 used. Think RuneScape's curve, where level 92 alone represents roughly half of level 99's total XP: early freeplay levels stay easy and quick, but the amount each successive level demands creeps up steadily, so very high levels are a real long-term investment without leveling itself ever becoming punishing. Proposed starting formula: XP required for freeplay level N (N counted from the first freeplay level onward) = FREEPLAY_BASE_XP + FREEPLAY_STEP * N^1.5, placeholder values base=300, step=60 -- smoothly rising, not a runaway exponential.

Freeplay levels alternate strictly between two kinds of rewards: odd freeplay levels (5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15...) unlock a brand-new pearl type; even freeplay levels (6, 8, 10, 12, 14...) let the player choose one upgrade category to add to their permanent shop. Level 5 doubles as both the freeplay-unlock moment and the first new pearl.

Pearl types: each tier is only a new mature-pearl sprite -- the growing dummy sprite never changes, so a clam's eventual pearl type stays a surprise until harvest.
- Classic Pearl (already built) -- Akoya-inspired, cream-white with a soft rose sheen, perfectly round. $200. Available from Level 1.
- Blush Pearl -- freshwater-inspired, soft pink-lavender tint, slightly baroque/uneven shape rather than a perfect sphere. $300. Unlocks Level 5.
- Tahitian Pearl -- dark charcoal-black base with peacock green and violet iridescent overtones, round. $450. Unlocks Level 7.
- Golden South Sea Pearl -- warm golden/champagne color, the largest and glossiest of the set. $650. Unlocks Level 9.
- Conch Pearl -- pink to salmon-orange with a distinctive wavy "flame" surface pattern (its real-world signature trait) and a less-spherical, slightly oval shape. $900. Unlocks Level 11.
- Melo Pearl -- rich orange-brown, extremely glossy, the largest and warmest-toned of the set, and one of the rarest pearls in reality. $1200. Unlocks Level 13.
- Abalone Pearl -- irregular, blister-like shape with rainbow blue-green-purple iridescent nacre. $1600. Unlocks Level 15.
That covers levels 5 through 15 (seven tiers total). Past Level 15, odd levels only give the upgrade pick (no new pearl). POSSIBLE LATER FEATURE (not being implemented now): "Flawless"/"Radiant" recolors of existing tiers (palette shift plus a sparkle overlay) to keep the every-other-level pearl cadence alive.

Each harvest rolls which pearl type comes out among every type currently unlocked, weighted so rarer/higher-value tiers are proportionally less likely -- e.g. with all seven unlocked: Classic 40%, Blush 22%, Tahitian 15%, Golden South Sea 10%, Conch 7%, Melo 4%, Abalone 2%. Weights re-normalize as new tiers unlock so the odds always total 100%.

Upgrades: the categories are new clam slot, faster pearl growth, bigger sonic flare radius, and a higher chum-meter threshold before crabs start spawning (read as: raises how much chum heat the meter tolerates before crab spawning begins, i.e. a defensive upgrade -- flag if a different effect was meant). That's four categories, not the three originally described, so it still needs deciding whether to drop one, always offer all four, or something else.
At each even freeplay level, the player picks one category, which becomes a card in the "Available to Buy" tab of an upgrades menu (opened via a dedicated toolbar button) until purchased. Because the same category can be picked again at a later level-up before an earlier pick of it is bought, an Available card tracks how many unpurchased picks of that category are pending rather than duplicating the card -- buying it consumes one pending pick and applies one stack of the effect immediately. The "Owned" tab shows the running total of stacks already purchased per category and their cumulative effect (e.g. "Growth Speed +6s total, 3 purchased"). Upgrades never expire or get overwritten by a later pick of the same or a different category.
Each stacking category needs its own ceiling so it can't trivialize the game: growth-speed reduction needs a floor (e.g. growth_time never below ~5s), flare radius needs a cap well short of the whole play area, and the chum threshold needs to stay meaningfully below CHUM_METER_MAX so crabs can still eventually spawn. New clam slots are self-limiting once the pond runs out of pre-placed slots (see below). Because later stacks make each upgrade more valuable, each purchase's dollar cost should probably scale up with how many stacks of that category are already owned, so the economy stays meaningful instead of flattening out once everything is affordable.

New clam slots: rather than spawning clams dynamically at runtime, pre-place a fixed, generous number of additional clam nodes around the pond at game start (e.g. 8-10 total slots), locked beyond the first 3. Each "new clam slot" purchase unlocks the next one in that pre-placed sequence, independent of player level -- this reuses the existing per-clam lock/unlock pattern instead of needing new spawn-and-layout logic.

Sonic flares: every level-up grants one free flare, except the level that first unlocks the sonic flare (Level 4, pre-freeplay), which grants five instead. Beyond the free grants, flares can be bought anytime once unlocked, at $30 each for now (to be tuned later). Since this is a repeatable currency purchase rather than a one-time unlock, it doesn't fit the Available/Owned tab structure -- a small "+ Buy" button right next to the sonar ammo counter on the toolbar is a more natural fit for quick buys than a trip into the upgrades menu, though the menu can still list it for discoverability.

Implementation risk to handle deliberately, not by accident: apply_level_unlocks() currently overwrites each clam's growth_time directly from the level-based array on every level-up. Once growth-speed upgrades exist, that overwrite would silently erase any upgrade-driven reduction on the next level-up unless growth_time is recomputed each time as (base growth time for level) minus (growth-upgrade stacks * seconds per stack), rather than being set once and left alone.

Other freeplay decisions worth making on purpose: whether crab difficulty (spawn rate or max crab count) should scale up somewhat with level, so the threat stays relevant once flare-radius and chum-threshold upgrades are stacked; and treating PlayerProgress's freeplay state (level, XP, money, owned upgrade stacks, unlocked pearl tiers, unlocked clam slots) as one clean serializable structure now, so the Save/Load buttons already sitting in the pause menu have something straightforward to eventually hook up to.

Additional feature ideas beyond what's planned above: a pearl "collection log" screen showing every tier ever harvested (cheap to build, reuses the pearl sprites already being made); idle/offline growth so clams keep progressing while the game isn't running, which fits a pearl-farming theme well; pond decoration purchases as a lightweight secondary money sink alongside the upgrade shop; and giving the Settings placeholder real options (volume, at minimum) now that a menu shell exists for it. A prestige/reset system for very late freeplay is possible but likely beyond this project's scope.

About crabs: Every use of chum will accumulate on the chum scale on the side of the UI (a vertical bar on the side, can be implemented later, use prints for now). Once a certain threshold on that bar is reached (let's say 3/10), then crabs start spawning. The higher the bar is, the higher the rate of spawns. The meter goes down with time, but really slowly. Crabs can spawn from both left and right side, anywhere on the y axis as long as its within the blue water area. Crab targeting logic - it always targets the clam that is closest to finishing its growth. Maximum crabs spawned at a time is 4. If a crab is walking towards a clam, but the player harvests the pearl before the crab gets there, the crab instantly changes direction and walks toward another growing clam. All crabs move at the same speed. When a crab gets to its target shell, it stays to the side of it, and a horizontal red bar appears above the pearl (it replaces the green horizontal bar above the clam that shows growing progress) to indicate the crab is messing with the pearl. After 2 seconds, when the red bar fills up, the pearl disappears and crab walks away off screen in 2x speed. The player has to replant the dummy pearl. If there was any sediment on the clam, it gets cleared with the pearl.

Tunable clam values:
- growth_time = 10.0s (time to mature from planted dummy to ready pearl)
- sediment_rate = 0.15 sediment/sec (accumulates only while state == GROWING)
- chum_boost = 1.0s

Growth / sediment formula actually implemented:
- Sediment only accumulates while GROWING; it's frozen while EMPTY or READY.
- growth_speed_multiplier = 1.0 - (sediment_level * 0.9). Growth never fully stops — at max sediment (1.0) it still creeps at 10% normal speed.
- growth_progress += delta * growth_speed_multiplier, each frame, only while GROWING.
- The sediment overlay sprite only becomes visible once sediment_level > 0.1 (and only while GROWING)

Chum is an instant flat progress jump, not a temporary speed boost. Clicking Chum on a GROWING clam adds chum_boost seconds directly to growth_progress once per click. It has no effect on EMPTY or READY clams.

Scrub Brush subtracts a flat 0.2 from sediment_level each click.

Tool/input architecture:
- Global.gd (autoload singleton) holds ONE global active_tool enum (HAND, BRUSH, CHUM, SONAR) and updates it from input actions (HAND_SELECT/BRUSH_SELECT/CHUM_SELECT/SONAR_SELECT — bound to 1/H, 2/B, 3/C, 4/F) via _unhandled_input.
- Each Clam is an Area2D that listens for input_event (a click landing on ITS OWN collision shape) and then matches Global.active_tool to decide what happens. This means Hand/Brush/Chum are all "click directly on a clam" tools.
- Sonar currently has a placeholder: clicking a clam while Sonar is selected just prints "Sonar used on clam! (No effect)" — there's no real area/targeting logic for it yet, and it's only wired to fire when a clam itself is clicked (not a click anywhere in the water).
- There is no signal system yet anywhere in the project — nothing broadcasts state changes (planted / harvested / matured); other systems would have to poll a clam's public vars to know what it's doing.

Leveling / XP:
- Micro-action XP (Level 1 through Level 4): plant = 2, harvest = 3, chum = 2. Scrubbing awards no XP (removed -- it was causing premature leveling). Selling awards 40 XP per pearl and is the dominant level-up driver, matching the "selling is the main driver" design goal.
- Levels 2 through 5 use a derived threshold, not hardcoded numbers: each level requires selling 1, 2, 3, and 3 pearls respectively (LEVEL_PEARLS_REQUIRED), and the XP-per-pearl-cycle (plant + harvest + sell = 2 + 3 + 40 = 45) is multiplied through that pearl count and accumulated -- so leveling up is only mathematically possible after actually selling the required pearls, not through grinding micro-actions alone.
- Growth times: Level 1 = 5s, Level 2 = 10s, Level 3 = 30s, Level 4+ = 30s, until freeplay growth-speed upgrades start reducing it further (see Stage 2).
- Freeplay (Level 6+) switches to an escalating-per-level XP curve rather than the pearl-count formula above -- see Stage 2 for the proposed formula.

Stage 2 decisions (confirmed 2026-09-22 -- these override anything above that conflicts):
- Freeplay starts at Level 5. There is no max level. The XP curve applies from the 5->6 step onward (N=1 for 5->6).
- Selling rarer pearls gives more money AND more XP (Classic stays 40 XP so the Stage 1 math is unchanged).
- A pearl's type is rolled when it matures (so the open READY clam shows the right sprite); the growing dummy never changes.
- Classic pearl sprite is akoya_pearl.png; mature_pearl.png is removed.
- Upgrade picks happen at EVERY freeplay level-up. On odd levels (5-15) the player sees two popups in order: new pearl first, then the upgrade pick.
- Upgrade pick: 3 randomly chosen upgrades of distinct types (from a growing pool -- currently 4 types: new clam slot, growth speed, flare radius, chum threshold; more will be added later). Player selects a card (highlighted border), then chooses "Save for Later" (goes to Available tab) or "Buy Now" (pays and applies immediately).
- Clam slots: max 6 total (3 from Stage 1 + 3 purchasable). Each slot has a pre-determined spot for now. FUTURE: the player will be able to place a new clam slot wherever they want.
- Sonic flares: start at 0, +5 at Level 4, +1 every level-up after, $30 each via a "+" button next to the ammo counter.
- Crab difficulty: every 3 levels past Level 5 (8, 11, 14...), max simultaneous crabs goes up by 1.
- Pause menu: Save, Load, Settings, Exit (Exit quits the game). Settings has toggles: infinite sonic flares, instant clean, instant chum.
- PlayerProgress state is serializable (to_dict/from_dict) and hooked to Save/Load.
- POSSIBLE LATER FEATURES (not now): Flawless/Radiant pearl recolors, pearl collection log, pond decoration purchases, idle/offline growth, free placement of clam slots.
